Travel Studio Privacy Policy

Version: 2026-11-09

Preamble

Travel Studio (the "Service") provides features for creating, editing, storing, sharing, publicly displaying, and collaboratively editing travel itineraries. The Service is provided by the operating entity identified on the "Operator Information" page of the Travel Studio website (the "Platform"). The Platform collects, processes, and uses user data in accordance with the Personal Data Protection Act of the Republic of China (Taiwan) and other applicable laws, following the principles of data minimization, access control, and purpose limitation.

Newly created itineraries are private by default. Public display, permission to copy, reuse of original content, and commercial promotion are each governed by the user's own settings and authorizations. A place a user "wants to go" in a plan is not a confirmed visit, and an estimated cost is not an actual expense.

Article 1. Data Controller, Outsourced Processing, and Contact

1. The Platform is the collector, processor, and user of personal data collected through the Service. Its legal name, registered address, contact email, and the procedure for exercising personal data rights are published on the "Operator Information" and "Contact Us" pages of the Service website. Users may make inquiries or submit personal data requests through those channels.

2. The Platform may engage third parties to perform system development and maintenance, website hosting, identity verification, database, photo storage, information security, backup, and other technical operations necessary to provide the Service. The Platform imposes contractual requirements on such processors regarding purpose, access, confidentiality, security, and deletion or return of data. Processors may not use identifiable personal data of users for any purpose outside the scope of their engagement.

3. Where a development or maintenance processor is separately authorized by contract to access or use aggregated statistical results that have been appropriately de-identified, such use is limited to performance analysis and quality improvement of the contracted product and to explicitly agreed technical research. The processor may not access identifiable itineraries, accounts, photos, notes, or raw event data for its own business, advertising, or data sales. Whether such access is granted, the data categories involved, and the contractual rights are governed by the formal engagement contract and applicable disclosure requirements. Absent authorization, the processor has no such rights.

Article 2. Purposes of Collection

The Platform collects, processes, and uses data for the following purposes:

  1. Providing itinerary creation, editing, storage, reading, sharing, and real-time multi-user collaboration.
  2. Account sign-in (Google, LINE, Apple, or email verification code), anonymous collaborator verification, account linking, permission management, and account administration.
  3. Managing places, accommodation, times, transport, budgets, bookings, photos, stamps, notes, and exports.
  4. Providing public itinerary browsing, likes ("hearts"), rankings, and itinerary copying.
  5. Recording service usage events for feature operation, version history, reminders, synchronization, troubleshooting, and service quality monitoring.
  6. Maintaining information security and preventing unauthorized access, spam, abusive reporting, and like manipulation.
  7. Handling reports, complaints, support requests, legal disputes, and legal obligations.
  8. Where permitted by applicable law and with the necessary authorization, analyzing de-identified travel behavior data to improve the Service and to produce trends and recommendations.
  9. Within the scope separately authorized by the user, publicly displaying itineraries, permitting copying, reusing original content, or using content for commercial promotion.

Data processing necessary to provide the basic Service is managed separately from optional statistics, public display, and other optional authorizations.

Article 3. Categories of Data Collected

1. Sign-in Account Data

No sign-in is required to browse the website or public itineraries. Users may first enter a destination and travel dates; sign-in is requested only when the user taps "Create trip." After sign-in, the user returns to the website and the trip is created without re-entering the form.

The Service supports the following sign-in methods. Depending on the method chosen and the scope actually authorized, the Platform may receive:

  1. Google: Google user ID, email address, display name, and avatar.
  2. LINE: LINE user ID, display name, and avatar; email address only if separately authorized by the user.
  3. Apple: Apple user ID, name (provided on first sign-in only), and email address or an Apple-provided relay address.
  4. Email verification code: email address and verification records.

This data is used for identity verification, account management, itinerary ownership, and security. The Service does not currently request access to Gmail, Google Drive, contacts, LINE friend lists, or message content. Data obtained through Google APIs is handled in accordance with the Google API Services User Data Policy; data obtained from LINE and Apple is handled in accordance with the LINE Developers and Apple developer terms respectively. The Platform does not sell sign-in data to data brokers or use it for unauthorized ad targeting.

When the same user signs in with different methods and the email addresses match, the Platform may link them to a single account. Users may also link or unlink sign-in methods in account settings. Accounts using an Apple relay address, or LINE accounts without an email address, must be linked manually by the user.

2. Anonymous Collaborator Data

A friend who receives a valid edit invitation may join by entering a nickname and confirming the required notices in a browser such as the one built into LINE. The system creates an anonymous authenticated identity, identifier, and session for that person and records their authorizations, actions, and edit history. A nickname is not reliable proof of identity; an anonymous user may still constitute identifiable personal data by virtue of their identifier and activity information.

An anonymous identity cannot create its own trips, publish a trip, generate invitation links, or like trips. An anonymous collaborator may choose to link any supported sign-in method; after linking, the existing identity, permissions, and edit history continue without creating a new account. In in-app browsers such as LINE's, Google and Apple sign-in may not be supported; users may sign in with LINE instead or follow the system's guidance to switch to a standard browser.

If an unlinked anonymous user clears browser data or loses their session and later rejoins through a valid invitation link, a new anonymous identity is created. Prior activity records are retained in accordance with this Policy; the trip owner may remove duplicate or unneeded collaborators.

3. Itinerary and Collaboration Data

This includes the trip name, country and city, travel dates, local time zone, number of travelers, trip origin and the trip it was copied from, places or restaurants, accommodation, location or map links, card type, order, start and end times, whether a time is fixed, duration, transport mode and estimated transport cost, estimated and actual expenses, currency, per-person or group cost, who paid, payment and booking status, reminder dates, booking confirmation details, photos, notes, custom stamp names and records, and version and collaborator activity records.

To reduce disclosure risk, booking confirmation codes, payment details, private notes, and identifying information about travel companions are not automatically disclosed when a trip is made public. Users should also avoid entering unnecessary sensitive personal information in free-text fields.

4. Actual Visit and Spending Records

From the trip's departure date, users may mark each card as "visited / skipped / unmarked (unknown)" and may optionally enter the actual amount spent. Unmarked items are not automatically treated as visited or skipped. The Platform distinguishes planned visits from actual reports and does not treat estimated costs as actual spending. Ratings, post-trip reviews, and post-trip photos are future features; notices will be updated before they are enabled.

5. Service Usage Events and Security Records

These include creating a trip, adding or moving items, setting times or costs, marking visits, copying or opening invitations, joining as a collaborator, publishing or unpublishing, liking, copying a trip, opening a share link, exporting data, and clicks on external links opened through the Platform's redirect (for example, maps, official websites, or booking URLs). They may also include event time, trip identifier, actor type, anonymity status, device type, necessary IP and browser information, and error and security logs.

The content fields of service usage events do not include email addresses, nicknames, notes, or other free text. However, where an event identifier can be linked to an account or trip, that record is still handled under personal data rules. Technical security logs may additionally contain information necessary to protect the Service.

Article 4. Period, Region, Recipients, and Method of Use

1. Period: the life of the account and trip, retention periods required by law or necessary for the stated purposes, and the deletion and backup rotation periods set out in Article 11.

2. Region: the Service may process data in Taiwan and in the locations of the overseas cloud data centers, backup facilities, or technology vendors it uses. The primary database is Supabase; the actual project data center region and outsourced processing locations are published on the "Data Processing Locations and Vendors" page of the Service website and will be disclosed before launch. Where international transfers occur, the Platform takes the measures required by the Personal Data Protection Act and related restrictions.

3. Recipients: authorized operations and maintenance personnel, processors necessary to deliver the Service, collaborators designated by the user, holders of valid view links, general visitors after the user chooses to publish, and authorities legally entitled to request data. Partner merchants do not receive users' private itineraries or identifiable account information merely by virtue of a partnership.

4. Method: through the website, identity verification, databases, cloud files, real-time synchronization, redirect services, access controls, and backup mechanisms, used within the stated purposes, applicable law, and the scope of authorization.

Article 5. Third-Party Services, Cookies, Local Storage, and Analytics

1. Identity verification: the Platform verifies accounts through Google OAuth, LINE Login, Sign in with Apple, and email verification codes, obtaining only the basic data listed in Article 3 within the permissions actually requested. Email verification codes are sent by an email delivery service engaged by the Platform, which processes only the recipient address and delivery status.

2. Supabase: provides authentication, database, photo storage, permission management, and multi-user synchronization. Data is stored in the Supabase project region selected by the Platform; the primary region is published under Article 4, and backup and cross-border processing are disclosed according to the actual vendor information.

3. Hosting, security, and analytics: may be provided by hosting and security vendors such as Cloudflare and by the traffic analytics tool the Platform formally enables. The vendors used, necessary cookies, and analytics types are listed on the "Data Processing Locations and Vendors" page; services not enabled are not described as enabled.

4. Maps and place services: when a user searches for a place or opens Google Maps or another external website, the third party may process connection information under its own policy. The Platform limits the storage and reuse of place data according to the data source and its terms of service.

5. LINE sharing and sign-in: users may share URLs through LINE on their own; this does not give the Platform access to LINE friend lists or message content. When signing in with LINE, the Platform obtains the basic LINE data described in Article 3 and does not obtain friend lists or message content. If LINE Official Account features are enabled in the future, separate notice will be given.

6. Cookies and browser storage: the Platform may use cookies, localStorage, and sessionStorage to store necessary sign-in and anonymous session credentials, language and color theme preferences, pending unsynced changes, and security settings. Clearing browser data may make an anonymous identity unrecoverable. If GA4 or other non-essential analytics tools are adopted, notice and the corresponding consent or opt-out management will be provided as required by law, and no email addresses, nicknames, booking confirmation codes, or full note text will be sent to analytics tools.

7. External link redirects: map, official website, and booking links may be opened through the Platform's redirect endpoint for URL safety checks and click statistics. The redirect does not deliberately append the user's identifier to external URLs; third parties may still obtain IP, browser, and other necessary technical information through the ordinary network connection.

Article 6. Statistics and Itinerary Authorizations (Itemized)

1. Processing Necessary to Provide the Service

The Platform may process relevant data as necessary to store, synchronize, and share the itineraries a user designates. Declining necessary processing may prevent use of features that require sign-in or editing.

2. De-identified Statistics and Service Improvement

After clearly informing the user and obtaining the user's separate consent to non-essential statistical use, the Platform may use appropriately processed information on places, time slots, estimated and actual expenses, transport, visits, and interactions to compile popular areas, improve features, analyze service quality, and provide baseline data for recommendations. Users may withdraw this consent in "Privacy Settings"; after withdrawal, new data is no longer included in these statistics. Declining or withdrawing does not affect basic itinerary editing or collaboration.

Statistical results that can still be reasonably linked back to a specific individual continue to be handled under personal data rules. Aggregated results that have been irreversibly de-identified and are no longer personal data under the law may be retained and used by the Platform within the original purposes; such processing is carried out on the basis of the notice in this Policy, with its scope and method determined by the Platform under applicable law.

3. Public Display

Trips default to "Private." The owner may choose "Anyone with the link" or "Public." Before publishing, the Platform provides a preview of the public content and requests confirmation of the public display authorization. Public display authorization does not permit other users to copy original content, nor does it authorize the Platform to use photos or original text in separately produced recommendation articles or advertising.

4. Allow Other Users to Copy

The Platform provides an independent "Allow other users to copy this trip" switch for public trips, enabled by default. The owner can clearly see it on the publishing screen and may turn it off at any time before or after publishing. When off, others can still view the public trip but cannot copy it with one click. A copied trip records its source. Collaborators, upon joining a trip, have agreed that content they add may be publicly displayed and copied along with the trip; the owner remains responsible for confirming that content provided by third parties (non-collaborators) is appropriately licensed.

5. Reuse in Platform Recommendations

Structured data from public trips (such as places, time slots, and expenses) may be included in statistics and recommendation analysis within the scope of the consent, de-identification, and lawful purposes described in Article 6.2; authorizing the Platform to reuse original photos or text is not a prerequisite.

If the Platform wishes to separately reproduce, edit, or publish a user's original photos, descriptions, or reviews on recommendation pages, promotional materials, or standalone content, it must obtain a separate optional authorization that identifies the material and its use. A user who declines this authorization may still publish their trip.

6. Commercial Promotion and Partnerships

If original content is to be used in paid advertising, brand promotion, or partner merchant promotion, the Platform must separately explain the material, use, channel, and duration and obtain the corresponding authorization. Declining does not affect the basic Service or public display. Partner merchants may not directly obtain identifiable private itinerary data unless there is a separate, clear legal basis.

7. Managing Authorizations and Withdrawal

The consents and authorizations above are recorded separately by purpose, version, and time of consent. Users may modify or withdraw optional uses through privacy or sharing settings. After withdrawal, the Platform ceases the corresponding subsequent use and handles existing data according to law and technical feasibility. Lawfully produced aggregated statistics that cannot be re-identified, and copies retained by third parties while content was public, may not be fully recoverable.

Article 7. Privacy in Public Trips and Collaboration

Collaborators may, within their permissions, see the schedule, accommodation, budget, and notes of a shared trip. Users should not enter sensitive information they do not want collaborators to see. View links and edit invitations carry different permissions; an invitation link may act as an edit credential and should be kept secure. The owner may revoke edit invitations, remove collaborators, or unpublish.

Public trips should mask booking confirmation codes, payment details, private notes, and other data unsuitable for disclosure. Third parties may nonetheless take screenshots while a trip is public, and unpublishing cannot guarantee retrieval of copies already saved externally.

Article 8. Data Security and Incident Notification

The Platform uses HTTPS, access controls, database access restrictions, invitation link protection, backups, security monitoring, and necessary audit measures. No online service can guarantee zero risk. In the event of theft, leakage, alteration, destruction, or other security incident involving data, the Platform will assess the impact, take necessary immediate response measures, and notify affected individuals and competent authorities (where legally required) in accordance with the laws of the Republic of China in force at the time of the incident.

Article 9. Data Subject Rights and How to Exercise Them

Under the Personal Data Protection Act of the Republic of China, users may request to inquire about or review their personal data, obtain copies, supplement or correct it, cease its collection, processing, or use, and delete it. Requests may be submitted through account settings, privacy settings, or the "Contact Us" channel. The Platform may first complete necessary identity or permission verification and will handle data involving other collaborators' rights in accordance with law. An anonymous user who cannot prove authority over an earlier session may be unable to operate the old account directly or to request changes to another person's shared trip.

Article 10. Consequences of Not Providing Data

Users who do not sign in may still browse public trips or join as anonymous collaborators via a valid invitation, but cannot create their own trips. When an anonymous collaborator attempts to like a trip, the system guides them to link a supported sign-in method rather than creating a new account. Without a nickname, anonymous joining is not possible. Omitting times, budgets, or visit status only leaves the related features incomplete; unmarked visits are always treated as unknown. Declining non-essential statistics, reuse of original content, or commercial promotion does not affect the basic Service; without public display authorization, a trip cannot be set to Public.

Article 11. Retention, Export, and Deletion

1. Registered accounts and trips: users may export their trips as JSON and request account deletion. After necessary identity verification, personal data in primary systems with no other lawful basis for retention is deleted within 30 days in principle, and public trips are taken down at the same time. Content within trips owned by others may be retained to the extent lawfully necessary, with account identifiers no longer needed deleted or de-identified.

2. Anonymous collaborator accounts: anonymous accounts that have not linked a sign-in method and have had no sign-in, edit, or other valid activity for 12 consecutive months enter the deletion process in principle. Whether active collaboration permissions and shared trip content must be retained is assessed according to necessity and applicable law. This period is the Platform's default management period; unless otherwise required by law, related data is handled under this Policy.

3. Backups: after the deletion process is completed, deleted data is cleared or overwritten within a backup rotation period not exceeding 90 days in principle, except where retention is necessary for legal obligations, unresolved disputes, or security incidents.

4. General operational and security logs: retained for 180 days in principle; logs related to incidents, legal disputes, or reports may be retained longer as necessary. Audit logs of administrative operations are kept separately from general logs, managed under the Platform's internal controls and legally required periods, and are not subject to the 180-day limit.

5. Service usage events: raw event records that can be linked to a specific account or trip are managed under the service delivery, security, and retention policies; after account deletion, such data is deleted or irreversibly de-identified where no other lawful basis for retention exists. Aggregated statistical results lawfully processed and de-identified under Article 6.2 may be retained within the original purposes.

6. Reports, appeals, and legally required data: retained for the period needed to handle disputes, prevent abuse, and meet legal requirements; deleted or no longer used once the purpose ceases or the period expires.

Article 12. Protection of Minors

The Service is intended primarily for persons with the corresponding legal capacity. Persons under 18 should have the necessary consent or assistance of a legal guardian as required by the laws of the Republic of China. Users should avoid publicly disclosing minors' detailed travel dates, accommodation locations, or other identifiable whereabouts. Where violations occur or reports involve minors' rights, the Platform will take appropriate measures under applicable law.

Article 13. Changes to This Policy

This Policy may be updated due to changes in features, data uses, or law. The Platform will publish the new version, its effective date, and any necessary summary of changes. Where processing involves new purposes or legally requires consent, the necessary consent will be obtained before implementation. For policy inquiries and rights requests, please use the officially published "Contact Us" channel on this website.

This Privacy Policy is published in Traditional Chinese and English. The English version is provided for reference only. In the event of any discrepancy or inconsistency between the two versions, the Chinese version shall prevail.
